🎱 RackUp Privacy Policy

Effective date: 1 October 2026 · Applies to the RackUp apps for Android and iPhone (com.rackup.app)

What changed on 1 October 2026. The iPhone app now has push notifications too, from version 2.1.0. If you allow them, it stores a Firebase Cloud Messaging token for your iPhone under your account, as the Android app already did, so we can tell you about join requests, match updates, reminders and alerts from halls you starred. It asks once, after you sign in and finish the welcome tour, and you can switch them off in Settings or in your iPhone's settings. Nothing else about what we store has changed.

Deleting your account now also deletes the notifications in other players' activity feeds that name you, such as the one telling a host you asked to join their match. Until this change they stayed in the other player's feed after you left. So that they can be found, each such notification now also stores the user id of the player it names. The version dated 4 September 2026 replaced the policy dated 7 July 2026. The old one covered only the Android app and said the app collected no analytics. That was wrong. Both apps include Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics, and the policy says so, along with everything else the apps actually store. Nothing about the app changed on 4 September 2026: the policy was corrected to match the app.

RackUp is an app for billiards players in Greece who want to find an opponent for a real game at a real venue. You post when and where you want to play, and another player takes the table. This page explains, in plain language, what data the app handles, who can see it, and what your rights are.

Who is responsible for your data

RackUp, Ioannina, Greece, is the data controller for the personal data described on this page. You can reach us at rackupbilliard@gmail.com about anything on this page, including any request about your rights.

What we collect, and why

Your account

To sign in you use an email address and a password, or Google (Android), or Sign in with Apple (iPhone). Passwords are handled and stored by Google Firebase Authentication and we never see them. If you sign in with Apple and choose to hide your address, we store the Apple private relay address exactly as Apple gives it to us.

Your profile record holds your display name, your email address, the skill category you choose for yourself (Pro down to E), a link to your profile photo if you have one, your notification preferences, your language, and a pointer to your most recent venue check-in. Only you can read your own profile record. We use it to run your account and to show your name and category on the matches you post or join.

Your profile photo (Android only)

If you set a profile photo on Android, the image is resized on your device and uploaded to Firebase Cloud Storage as avatars/<your user id>.jpg. Any signed in player can view it, because it is shown next to your matches. The iPhone app has no photo picker at all, so no photo can be uploaded from an iPhone, and a photo set on Android cannot be removed from an iPhone.

What you post and write

The app is a noticeboard, so a lot of what it stores is content you create on purpose:

  • Match posts and open challenges: the venue, city, game type, date and time, and any free text note you write, together with your display name, skill category, photo and reliability figure copied onto the post. Every signed in player can read these. That is the point of the app.
  • Join requests: when you ask to join someone's match, your name, category and photo go to that host. Only you and the host can read the request.
  • Results and disputes: the scores either player reports, whether the other player confirmed or contested them, no show reports, and whether a no show was contested. These sit on the match record and are readable by signed in players.
  • Cancellations: the reason you pick and any note you add, on the match record.
  • Post match ratings: a one to five star rating and fixed tags you give an opponent after a confirmed result. A rating is stored against the player it is about and only that player, and you, can read it.
  • Venue check-ins: if you check in at a venue, we store your user id and the time. There is no coordinate and no location check, and since 27 August 2026 only you can read your own check-ins. Check-in records written by older Android versions may still contain your display name.
  • Saved searches, starred venues and your weekly schedule: private to you. Note that an enabled schedule slot posts a public match on your behalf at the time you set.

Free text you type into a name, a match note, a challenge note, a cancellation note or the feedback form is checked against a profanity list on your device before it is sent.

Safety: reports and blocks

If you report a player, we store your user id, their user id, the match in question and what you wrote. Reports go into a collection that no app can ever read back. Only we can read them, in the Firebase console, and we do so to act on abuse. If you block a player, we store their user id in a private list of yours. Blocking is invisible to the person blocked, and it simply hides their posts from your feed.

Feedback

The feedback form in Settings sends us your user id, the category you pick, your message, the app version and build, and which platform you are on. Like reports, no app can read this back.

Notifications

We store a Firebase Cloud Messaging token for each of your devices, Android or iPhone, along with your language, so we can send you push notifications about join requests, match updates, reminders, saved search alerts and, for halls you starred, new games and check-ins there. You control these with the switches in Settings and with your device's notification permission. The iPhone app asks for that permission once, after you sign in and finish the welcome tour, and stores a token only if you allow it; its notifications are delivered through Apple's Push Notification service. Signing out removes the token for that device, and deleting your account removes all of them.

Crash diagnostics (both apps)

Both apps include Firebase Crashlytics. When the app crashes or hits a handled error, Crashlytics sends Google a report containing the crash, your device model, your operating system version and the app version. We use it to find and fix bugs. We never attach your account to a crash report. The app never calls Crashlytics' user id function, so reports carry only Crashlytics' own installation identifier, which we cannot match to a person.

App usage analytics (both apps)

Both apps include Firebase Analytics, and it is on by default. The old version of this policy said the app had no analytics profiles. That was wrong, and correcting it was the main reason for the version dated 4 September 2026.

We log twelve events about how the app is used: match_posted, join_requested, request_accepted, score_confirmed, notifications_opened, notification_row_tapped, saved_search_created, h2h_opened, h2h_rematch, opponent_rated, reputation_opened and score_disputed. Between them they carry two pieces of detail, both on match_posted: the game type, and whether the post was a "play now" post. No name, no email, no user id, no venue and no free text is ever attached to an analytics event. Firebase Analytics also collects its own standard measurements, such as app opens and sessions, and it derives approximate geography from a masked IP address.

Analytics is not linked to your account. The app never calls Analytics' user id function, so Analytics keys on its own per installation identifier. One practical consequence, stated honestly: because these records are not tied to your account, we cannot pick out an individual person's analytics records, and there is no in-app switch to turn analytics off. Uninstalling the app stops collection.

Location

On iPhone the app never touches location. It requests no location permission, contains no location code and carries no location usage description.

On Android the app can ask for the optional coarse location permission for one purpose: sorting the venue list by how near each venue is to you. That calculation happens on your phone, against venue coordinates the app already has. Your position is never sent to us and never stored anywhere. You can refuse the permission and the app works normally, just without distance sorting. Checking in at a venue is on the honour system and is not verified against your location.

What we do not do

  • No advertising. There is no ad SDK of any kind in either app.
  • No advertising identifier. The Android app strips the AD_ID permission that Firebase Analytics would otherwise pull in. The iPhone app links the version of Firebase Analytics built without advertising ID support, shows no App Tracking Transparency prompt, and does not read the IDFA.
  • No tracking across other companies' apps or websites. There is no attribution SDK, no data broker and no third-party analytics product beyond Google Firebase.
  • We do not sell your data, and we do not pass it to advertisers or data brokers.
  • No contacts, no health data, no payments. The app is free, has no in-app purchases and reads nothing from your address book.
  • No chat, no direct messages and no inbox. Nothing in the app is a private channel between two players.
  • No precise location on either platform.

Who can see what

  • Private to you: your email address, your profile record, your blocked list, your saved searches, your starred venues, your weekly schedule, your activity feed, the ratings you have received and your venue check-ins.
  • Visible to every signed in player: your display name, your skill category, your profile photo, your reliability figure and verified badge, and everything on the matches and challenges you post or join, including your notes, scores, dispute status and cancellation notes.
  • Visible only to us: abuse reports and feedback messages.
  • Nobody else. There is no public web profile and no part of the app is readable without signing in.

One consequence worth knowing: because match records pair a user id with a display name and are readable by any signed in player, a user id that appears anywhere in the app can be resolved to a display name by another player.

Where your data is stored, and who processes it

RackUp runs entirely on Google Firebase, on Google Cloud infrastructure. Google acts as our service provider and processes this data on our instructions. The services we use are:

  • Firebase Authentication: your sign-in identity and password.
  • Cloud Firestore: everything described above that is stored as a record.
  • Cloud Storage for Firebase: profile photos, uploaded from Android only.
  • Cloud Functions: our server-side jobs, including notifications, saved search alerts and the account erasure job. These run in Google's europe-west1 region.
  • Firebase Crashlytics: crash and diagnostic reports.
  • Firebase Analytics: the app usage events described above.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging: push notifications on both apps. On iPhone, Firebase passes them to Apple's Push Notification service, which delivers them.

Our database, storage and server functions are configured to run in Google's European regions. Crashlytics and Analytics are global Google services, so the diagnostic and usage data they collect may be processed outside the European Economic Area under Google's own data processing terms. Google's documentation for all of this is at firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

Data is encrypted in transit. Access from the apps is controlled by Firebase security rules that enforce exactly the visibility described in the section above.

Our lawful basis (GDPR Article 6)

  • Performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b)): creating and running your account, publishing your match posts, handling join requests, recording results, and everything else you need for the app to do its job.
  • Legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)): keeping RackUp safe and working. This covers abuse reports, blocking, no show and dispute records, the profanity filter, crash diagnostics, and the app usage analytics described above. Our interest is in running a service that is safe for players and does not crash; we have kept the data to the minimum that serves it, and none of the analytics or crash data is tied to your account. You can object to this processing at any time, at the address below.
  • Consent (Article 6(1)(a)): push notifications on both apps, which depend on your device permission and on the switches in Settings, and the optional Android location permission. You can withdraw either at any time in your device settings.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and everything attached to it: for as long as your account exists. Delete your account and the erasure described below runs.
  • Abuse reports: reports about you are kept as a safety record even after you delete your account, because a moderation history that vanishes on request is not a moderation history.
  • Crash and analytics records: held by Google under the Firebase project's own retention settings. They are not linked to your account, so they are not affected by account deletion and we cannot single out an individual's records.
  • Erasure records: when an account is erased we keep one small record holding the user id, a status, timestamps and the names of the steps that ran. It holds no name, no email, no photo and no message text. It is our evidence that the erasure happened, and no app can read it.

Deleting your account

In the app: Profile → Settings → Delete account → Yes, delete everything. If it has been a while since you signed in, Firebase will ask you to sign out and sign back in first. If you signed in with Apple, the app also asks Apple to revoke RackUp's access to your Apple Account.

Without the app: email rackupbilliard@gmail.com from your registered address with the subject "Account deletion request" and we will do it for you.

What deletion removes

Your sign-in account is deleted immediately. That automatically starts a server-side job which removes:

  • your profile record, and with it your name, email, category and photo link;
  • your profile photo file in Cloud Storage;
  • your notification tokens, notification preferences, activity feed, saved searches, starred venues, weekly schedule and blocked list;
  • the ratings other players gave you;
  • every match and every challenge you posted, along with the requests other players made to join them;
  • every join request and challenge claim you made on someone else's post;
  • the notifications in other players' activity feeds that name you, such as the one telling a host you asked to join their match;
  • your venue check-ins;
  • your feedback messages;
  • the abuse reports you filed about other people.

The job normally finishes within moments. If a step fails, a scheduled task retries the remaining steps every six hours until they are done.

What survives deletion, and why

We would rather be exact here than reassuring, so please read this part.

  • Matches you only joined stay on the host's record. The post belongs to the other player, so we do not destroy it. It keeps the display name, category and user id you had at the time. Your photo file is deleted, so the picture stops loading. If you want your name removed from those records too, email us and we will do it.
  • Ratings you wrote about other players stay, because they are the other player's reputation and that player did not ask for anything. We sever the link to you: the author field is overwritten with the fixed value [erased], which is the same value for everyone, so your ratings cannot be picked out or grouped afterwards.
  • Open challenges you claimed but did not host stay, with your name and id cleared, and the slot reopens.
  • Abuse reports filed about you stay, as a safety record. No app can read them.
  • The erasure record described under retention stays.
  • Server logs may contain your user id for a bounded period.

We do not claim that deleting your account erases every trace of you everywhere. The list above is what remains.

Your rights (GDPR)

If you are in the EU or the EEA you have the following rights over your personal data, and we will honour them all:

  • Access: ask us for a copy of the personal data we hold about you.
  • Rectification: have inaccurate data corrected. You can edit your name, category and photo in the app yourself.
  • Erasure: have your data deleted, as described above.
  • Portability: receive your data in a structured, machine readable format, or have us send it to another controller where that is technically feasible.
  • Objection: object to processing we carry out on the basis of legitimate interests, including the analytics and crash reporting described above.
  • Restriction: ask us to stop processing your data while a dispute about it is resolved.
  • Withdraw consent: turn off notifications or the Android location permission at any time, without affecting anything we did before you did so.

To exercise any of these, email rackupbilliard@gmail.com from the address on your account. We reply within one month, as the GDPR requires. There is no charge.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your data, you can complain to the Hellenic Data Protection Authority at dpa.gr, or to the supervisory authority in the EU country where you live.

Children

RackUp is not directed at children. The app arranges meetings between adults at billiard halls, and we do not knowingly collect data from children under 16. There is no age verification in the app. If you believe a child has created an account, email us and we will remove it.

Security

All traffic between the apps and Firebase is encrypted. What each account may read and write is enforced by Firebase security rules on the server, not by the app, so a modified app cannot reach data it is not entitled to. The Android app additionally verifies itself with Firebase App Check. We are a small team and we do not pretend to be immune to every risk, but if a breach affects your personal data we will notify the Hellenic Data Protection Authority and, where the law requires it, you.
