RackUp is an app for billiards players who want to find an opponent for a real game at a real venue. You post when and where you want to play, and another player takes the table. This page explains, in plain language, what data the app handles, who can see it, and what your rights are.

Who is responsible for your data

RackUp, Ioannina, Greece, is the data controller for the personal data described on this page. You can reach us at rackupbilliard@gmail.com about anything on this page, including any request about your rights.

What we collect, and why

Your account

To sign in you use an email address and a password, or Google (Android), or Sign in with Apple (iPhone). Passwords are handled and stored by Google Firebase Authentication and we never see them. If you sign in with Apple and choose to hide your address, we store the Apple private relay address exactly as Apple gives it to us.

Your profile record holds your display name, your email address, the skill category you choose for yourself (Pro down to E), your country and city, your usual hall and the games you play if you gave them, a link to your profile photo if you have one, your notification preferences, whether you appear in player search, your language, which version of the app you last used and on which platform, and a pointer to your most recent venue check-in. Only you can read your own profile record. We use it to run your account, to show your name and category on the matches you post or join, to build your player card, described below, and to keep the games, halls, leaderboards and most notifications you get to your own country. For an older profile with no city, the country is the app's default one. The app version also tells us how many players have updated: together with the time your app last refreshed its notification token, described under Notifications, it lets us count, as totals, how many of the players who opened the app recently run each version. Week by week, we count how many players signed up, by city and platform, and how many of them, in their first week, posted a match or a challenge, asked to join a match, claimed or accepted a challenge, or said they were looking for a game. We also count how many games and looking-for-a-game posts find a player, from the posts, join requests and challenge claims, and from the activity feed entries that tell a player about a game that fits their post. We look only at those totals, never at a single player, to judge our social media posts and ads and to see whether games fill.

Looking around as a guest

From version 2.3.0 of the apps, you can look around either app before you create an account: tap Look around first on the welcome screen. The app then signs in to Firebase Authentication without any details from you, which Firebase calls anonymous sign-in. That gives the app on your phone a random guest identifier: a user id with no name, no email address, no password and no profile. Firebase Authentication keeps it, with when it was created and when it was last used. The app needs it because our database lets only signed in sessions read. Earlier versions have no guest mode, and everything in this section applies from version 2.3.0.

As a guest you can see the open games in Discover, match pages, looking-for-a-game posts and the halls with their open games, with each player's name, category and photo, and the hall, game and time. You cannot see what a host wrote: the app shows the note on a match or a challenge, and the note on a cancelled match, only to players with an account. On a match page a guest sees the line "The host's note is for players with an account" instead. The note is also left out of a guest's Add to calendar entry. Versions 2.3.0 to 2.3.2 read the match and challenge records themselves and hide the note on screen, and there a guest can also open a game that has been taken or called off, with the reason it was called off. From version 2.3.3, the app reads only the copies of open games described below, which never hold a note, so a game that has been taken or called off shows as no longer there. On every version, our security rules still let any signed in session, a guest's included, read a whole match or challenge record, note included, if it asks our database directly. You cannot open player cards or search for players. Posting, asking to join, following, blocking, starring a hall, checking in, saving a search, challenging a player and notifications all need an account, and the app asks you to create one instead. Our security rules also stop a guest from creating a profile, uploading a photo, posting a match or a challenge, or checking in.

Copies of open games for guests. Our server keeps a copy of each open match and each open challenge for guests to read. A copy holds the host's user id, name, category, photo link and verified badge, the hall (its id, name, city, country and time zone), the game and the time. A match's copy also holds the host's reliability figure, the hall's address, the race, whether it is a play now post, when it was posted, and a yes or no for whether the host wrote a note. A challenge's copy also holds when it was posted and the range of categories it is open to. A copy never holds the note, a cancellation note, an opponent, a score or a no show. A copy is deleted as soon as its game is taken, called off or deleted, and when its host deletes their account. Otherwise our server deletes a match's copy within about an hour once the game is more than 2 hours past its start, and a challenge's copy within about an hour once it is 24 hours old or more than 2 hours past its time. Any signed in session can read the copies, and from version 2.3.3 the apps show them to guests.

We keep no profile record, no player card and no notification token for a guest. A guest can report a player from a match page, from a looking-for-a-game post or from a challenge in Discover. The report is stored as described under Safety: reports and blocks, with the guest identifier where an account's report carries its user id. A guest can also send us feedback from the app. It is stored as described under Feedback, with the guest identifier where an account's feedback carries its user id. When you choose to look around, Firebase Analytics records the event guest_browse_started, with no detail, and from then on it records a guest's use of the app as it does a player's, described under app usage analytics below, keyed on its own per installation identifier, not on the guest identifier.

When you create an account or sign in, you use that account, which has its own user id. Your guest identifier is not merged into it. How long guest identifiers are kept is under How long we keep it, below.

Your profile photo

You can set a profile photo on Android and, from version 2.2.0, on iPhone. You pick it with your phone's own photo picker, which gives the app only the photo you choose and no access to the rest of your library. The app scales it on your phone to at most 512 pixels on its longest side, saves it as a JPEG and uploads it to Firebase Cloud Storage as avatars/<your user id>.jpg. A link to it goes on your profile and your player card. Any signed in player can view it, and from version 2.3.0 so can a guest, because it is shown next to your matches and on your player card. Removing the photo in the app deletes the file, from either app, and so does deleting your account. If a player's photo is offensive, you can report them from their player card.

Your player card and player search

Each player with an account has a player card, which our server has kept since 1 October 2026 and which other players with an account can open from version 2.2.0 of the apps. It is a separate record, playerCards/<your user id>, which only our server writes, copying from your profile: your display name, your skill category, your country and city, your usual hall, the link to your profile photo, the games you play, whether you appear in player search, and a yes or no for whether your app is new enough to receive direct challenges. That yes or no is worked out from the app version on your profile; the version itself is not copied. For search, the card also holds the beginnings of each word of your name, in Greek and in Greeklish, without accents. Your card never holds your email address, your language, your notification settings, your push tokens or your app version.

The card screen also shows a player's wins and losses this month, their win rate, reliability figure and verified badge, and the viewer's own record against them. The viewer's phone works these out from match records that every signed in player could already read; none of them is stored on the card.

Player search is only for players with an account. It finds a player when what you type matches the start of any word of their name, in Greek or Greeklish, ignoring accents. You type at least 2 letters and see at most 20 players. Email addresses and cities are not searchable. Before you type, it suggests players you played with recently, taken from your own matches, and regulars at your usual hall. Players you blocked never appear.

Being findable is on by default. To stop it, turn off Show me in player search in Settings. That takes your name out of search and you out of the regulars at your hall. Your card itself stays readable: players you have played with can still open it, for example from their Played with recently list or from a match's quick messages, and you still appear on your matches, challenges and leaderboards.

What you post and write

The app is a noticeboard, so a lot of what it stores is content you create on purpose:

  • Match posts and open challenges: the venue, its city and country, the game type, date and time, and any free text note you write, together with your display name, skill category, photo and reliability figure copied onto the post. Every signed in player can read these. That is the point of the app. From version 2.3.0 a guest sees them too, but not your note, which the app shows only to players with an account; our security rules would still let a guest session read it.
  • Direct challenges: when you challenge one player by name, we store both players' user ids, display names, categories, photo links and verified badges, the game type, the hall (its id, name, city, country and time zone), the date and time you propose, any note you write (up to 500 characters), whether the challenge is waiting, accepted, declined, called off or expired, when it was sent, answered and due to expire, and, once accepted, the match it became. Only the two of you can read it, note included. If it is accepted, an ordinary match is created, which every signed in player can read, but the note is not copied onto it: it stays on the challenge, visible only to the two of you, and is deleted with the challenge, 30 days after its 24 hour answer window ends.
  • Join requests: when you ask to join someone's match, your name, category and photo go to that host. Only you and the host can read the request.
  • Quick messages: once a match is confirmed, its two players can send each other messages from a fixed list of eleven, such as "On my way" or "I'm at table 4". Nobody can type their own text. For each message we store the sender's and the recipient's user ids, which of the eleven it is, the table number from 1 to 40 for "I'm at table", and when it was sent. When you open the messages, we store a read mark for you, so the sender sees "Read" under the messages you have seen. Messages can be sent until two days after the match's start time, and never on a cancelled match. Only the two players can read them.
  • Results and disputes: the scores either player reports, whether the other player confirmed or contested them, no show reports, and whether a no show was contested. These sit on the match record and are readable by signed in players.
  • Cancellations: the reason you pick and any note you add, on the match record.
  • Post match ratings: a one to five star rating and fixed tags you give an opponent after a confirmed result. A rating is stored against the player it is about and only that player, and you, can read it.
  • Venue check-ins: if you check in at a venue, we store your user id and the time. There is no coordinate and no location check, and since 27 August 2026 only you can read your own check-ins. A check-in counts for 3 hours, and our server deletes it within about two hours after that. Your profile record keeps which hall you last checked in at, and when, until your next check-in or check-out. Check-in records written by older Android versions may still contain your display name.
  • Looking for a game: if you say you are looking for a game, we store a post with the game, the day, the part of the day and either a hall or just your city, with its country, together with your display name, skill category and photo. Every signed in player can read it, and from version 2.3.0 so can a guest. You can have one at a time. It is deleted when you withdraw it, and otherwise automatically, normally within two hours after that part of the day has ended.
  • Players you follow: if you follow a player, we store whom you follow and when. Only you can read your list, and the player you follow is not told. When they post a new open match, you get an entry in your activity feed and a push.
  • Saved searches, starred venues and your weekly schedule: private to you. A saved search and a schedule slot also hold the country of their city or hall; a saved search with neither holds the country on your profile. Note that an enabled schedule slot posts a public match on your behalf at the time you set.

Free text you type into a name, a match note, a challenge note, a cancellation note or the feedback form is checked against a profanity list on your device before it is sent.

Safety: reports and blocks

If you report a player, from a match or from their player card for example, we store your user id, their user id, the match or post in question if there is one, and what you wrote. Reports go into a collection that no app can ever read back. Only we can read them, in the Firebase console, and we do so to act on abuse. If you block a player, we store their user id in a private list of yours. Blocking is invisible to the person blocked. It hides their posts from your feed and keeps them out of your player search, and it hides their quick messages and direct challenges, which also stop notifying you.

Feedback

The feedback form in Settings sends us your user id, the category you pick, your message, the app version and build, and which platform you are on. Like reports, no app can read this back.

Notifications

We store a Firebase Cloud Messaging token for each of your devices, Android or iPhone, along with your language, the platform, the app version and build, and the time it was last updated, which the app refreshes each time it starts, so we can send you push notifications about join requests, match updates, direct challenges, quick messages, reminders, saved search alerts, new games from players you follow and, for halls you starred, new games and check-ins there. With the app version, that time also lets us count updates as totals, as described under Your account. You control notifications with the switches in Settings and with your device's notification permission, with two exceptions to the switches: a no-show report against you reaches you even with Match updates off, because it affects your verified badge, and a starred hall's alerts follow the star. The Android app stores its token whether or not you allow notifications. The iPhone app asks for that permission once, after you sign in and finish the welcome tour, and stores a token only if you allow it, then keeps it until you sign out, even if you later turn notifications off in the iPhone's settings; its notifications are delivered through Apple's Push Notification service. Signing out removes the token for that device, and deleting your account removes all of them.

A direct challenge adds an entry to the invited player's activity feed and sends them a push naming the sender, the hall and the time; the answer adds an entry and a push for the sender. A quick message comes only as a push, showing the sender's name and the message in your language, and adds nothing to your activity feed. The pushes for both follow the Match updates switch.

Crash diagnostics (both apps)

Both apps include Firebase Crashlytics. When the app crashes or hits a handled error, Crashlytics sends Google a report containing the crash, your device model, your operating system version and the app version. We use it to find and fix bugs. We never attach your account to a crash report. The app never calls Crashlytics' user id function, so reports carry only Crashlytics' own installation identifier, which we cannot match to a person.

App usage analytics (both apps)

Both apps include Firebase Analytics, and it is on by default. The old version of this policy said the app had no analytics profiles. That was wrong, and correcting it was the main reason for the version dated 4 September 2026.

We log 24 events about how the app is used, the same on both apps, or 23 before version 2.3.0, which adds guest_browse_started. Most of them record only that something happened. Here they all are, with any detail an event carries:

  • Signing in and the first run: sign_up and login, each with the sign-in method (password, Google or Apple); guest_browse_started, from version 2.3.0, when you choose to look around as a guest; email_verified; tutorial_complete, when you finish or skip the welcome tour; home_venue_set, when you pick a home hall at its end; and notification_permission_result, with whether you allowed notifications.
  • Matches: match_posted, with the game type, whether it was a "play now" post and the id of the hall; join_requested; request_accepted; score_reported; score_confirmed; score_disputed; opponent_rated; h2h_opened and h2h_rematch, when you open the head to head screen and when you tap its rematch button; and reputation_opened, when you open your own reputation screen.
  • Looking for a game: looking_for_game_posted, with the game type, the part of the day, whether any hall will do and whether it replaced an earlier post of yours; and looking_for_game_converted, when a match is posted from another player's looking-for-a-game post, with the game type and whether that post would take any hall.
  • Everything else: venue_checked_in, with the id of the hall; player_followed; saved_search_created; and notifications_opened and notification_row_tapped, when you open your activity feed and when you tap one of its entries.

The only ids any event carries are hall ids, on match_posted and venue_checked_in. A hall id names a billiard hall in our directory, not a person. No name, no email, no user id, no match id and no free text is ever attached to an analytics event. Firebase Analytics also collects its own standard measurements, such as app opens and sessions, and it derives approximate geography from a masked IP address.

Where new players come from. On Android, Google Play passes Firebase Analytics the tags of the link that led to an install, and when that link was tapped. The tags name a source, a medium and a campaign, and we choose them by where the link appears: for example our website, our Facebook Page, an Instagram link, an ad, a QR code on a poster or a match link another player shared. For our ads on Facebook and Instagram, the tags also say where the ad was shown, for example in the Facebook feed or in Instagram Reels. An install from a search in Google Play gets Play's own tag instead. We read these only as totals, never player by player: how many first opens of the app, and how many sign-ups, followed each kind of link. That shows us which of our social media posts, ads and posters bring new players. On iPhone the app collects nothing for this. Apple tells us only totals, such as how many downloads came from each of our campaign links, from our ads on the App Store, or from another app or website.

Analytics is not linked to your account. The app never calls Analytics' user id function, so Analytics keys on its own per installation identifier. One practical consequence, stated honestly: because these records are not tied to your account, we cannot pick out an individual person's analytics records, and there is no in-app switch to turn analytics off. Uninstalling the app stops collection.

Location

Both apps can ask for your approximate location, never your precise one, for one purpose: showing the halls and games near you first. The app asks only after you tap the card at the top of the Venues tab that offers to show the venues closest to you first or, from version 2.3.0, choose the Nearest sort there, and it reads your location only while you are using it. The iPhone app asks from version 2.2.0; the Android app, through its coarse location permission, has asked since its first versions.

The app uses your position on your phone, against venue coordinates it already has. From the hall nearest to you, the app works out your city. It orders every list of halls and games with your starred halls first, then your city, then everything else, nearest first, and it opens Discover on that city. The app never sends your phone's position to us, and we never store it. On Android, if you allowed location, the Venues map opens on your area, so Google Maps loads the map around you. The city worked out from your position orders what you see and is never written to your profile or to any post. If you save a search from Discover without changing its city, the saved search keeps that city. If you say no, nothing else changes: the lists put the city on your profile first instead, and the app works normally. Checking in at a venue is on the honour system and is not verified against your location.

From version 2.4.0 of the apps, to choose which country's games a guest sees and which country sign-up suggests, the Android app looks at the country of the mobile network the phone is on, else of its SIM card, else of the phone's language; the iPhone app uses your Region setting. None of this needs a permission. The app does not send us the network, SIM or language it reads: a guest's app only asks our database for that country's games, and your profile keeps only the country you confirm.

The location linked to your account is what you give us. The city you choose for your profile, which sign-up asks for, and the country it is in are on your profile record and your player card, described above, and the weekly sign-up counts are taken by city. We count them as approximate location, linked to your account. We also keep the halls you name yourself: your usual hall, the halls you starred, which bring you their notifications (on Android by signing your device up with Firebase Cloud Messaging, on iPhone through your account), your check-ins, and the hall or city of your posts, challenges, looking-for-a-game posts, saved searches and schedule, each described above.

What we do not do

  • No advertising in the apps. There is no ad SDK of any kind in either app.
  • No advertising identifier. The Android app strips the AD_ID permission that Firebase Analytics would otherwise pull in. The iPhone app links the version of Firebase Analytics built without advertising ID support, shows no App Tracking Transparency prompt, and does not read the IDFA.
  • No tracking across other companies' apps or websites. There is no attribution SDK, no data broker and no third-party analytics product beyond Google Firebase.
  • We do not sell your data, and we do not pass it to advertisers or data brokers.
  • No contacts, no health data, no payments. The app is free, has no in-app purchases and reads nothing from your address book.
  • No free text chat. Players cannot type messages to each other. The two players of a confirmed match can send only the fixed quick messages described above, and the only text you can write to a single player is the optional note on a direct challenge.
  • No precise location on either platform.

Who can see what

  • Private to you: your email address, your profile record, your blocked list, your saved searches, your starred venues, your weekly schedule, your activity feed, the ratings you have received, your venue check-ins and the list of players you follow. A player you follow is not told.
  • Private to you and one other player: a direct challenge between the two of you, and the quick messages and read marks of a confirmed match between the two of you.
  • Visible to every player with an account: your player card, described above, with your display name, skill category, country and city, usual hall, profile photo and the games you play. Unless you turn off Show me in player search, they can also find you by name, and players with the same usual hall see you among its regulars.
  • Visible to every signed in player: your display name, your skill category, your profile photo, your reliability figure and verified badge, everything on the matches and open challenges you post or join, including your notes, scores, dispute status and cancellation notes, and your looking-for-a-game post. From version 2.3.0, guests see this too, except the notes, which the app shows only to players with an account. On versions 2.3.0 to 2.3.2 a guest's lists show only games nobody has taken, and a played match only when the guest opens its link. From version 2.3.3, a guest's app shows only open games, from the copies described above, so no scores, results or cancellations either. Our security rules would still let a guest session read a note, as they let any signed in session read a whole match record. A guest is someone looking around in the app without an account, as described above.
  • Visible only to us: abuse reports and feedback messages.
  • Nobody without a sign-in, with one exception. There is no public web profile. Looking around as a guest, from version 2.3.0, still signs in, anonymously, as described above. With no sign-in at all, our database lets nothing be read except a settings record that tells each app the oldest version we still support, which holds no personal data. The exception is your profile photo: its link carries its own access key, so anyone who has the link can open the photo without signing in. That link is copied onto your posts, which every signed in player can read (and, from version 2.3.0, every guest), and onto your player card.

For reading, our security rules check that a request is signed in, not that it comes from an account. A guest session, which the apps start from version 2.3.0, is signed in that way, so it may read what every signed in player can read, listed above, notes included, except player cards, and nothing that is private to you or visible only to us. In a list, a guest session gets only games that nobody has taken, never a player's played matches. No version of the apps shows a guest a note.

One consequence worth knowing: because match records pair a user id with a display name and are readable by any signed in player or guest, a user id that appears anywhere in the app can be resolved to a display name by another player, or from version 2.3.0 by a guest. A player with an account can also read the player card behind it.

Where your data is stored, and who processes it

RackUp runs entirely on Google Firebase, on Google Cloud infrastructure. Google acts as our service provider and processes this data on our instructions. The services we use are:

How long we keep it

  • Your account and everything attached to it: for as long as your account exists. Delete your account and the erasure described below runs.
  • Guest identifiers, from version 2.3.0: kept in Firebase Authentication until we delete them. We do not delete them automatically today, and creating an account does not delete yours. A guest identifier holds no name, email address or profile, only when it was created and last used. Because nothing in it names you, we cannot pick yours out from a request by email.
  • Copies of open games for guests: deleted as soon as the game is taken, called off or deleted, or its host deletes their account, and otherwise within about an hour once a match is more than 2 hours past its start, or a challenge is 24 hours old or more than 2 hours past its time.
  • Direct challenges: one that is not answered within 24 hours expires. Every direct challenge, answered or not, is deleted 30 days after its 24 hours ran out. An accepted one has already become a match, which is kept like any other, without the challenge's note.
  • Quick messages: a daily job deletes each message once it is 14 days old, and each read mark at the same time as the newest message it marks.
  • Abuse reports: reports about you are kept as a safety record even after you delete your account, because a moderation history that vanishes on request is not a moderation history.
  • Crash and analytics records: held by Google under the Firebase project's own retention settings. They are not linked to your account, so they are not affected by account deletion and we cannot single out an individual's records.
  • Erasure records: when an account is erased we keep one small record holding the user id, a status, timestamps and the names of the steps that ran. It holds no name, no email, no photo and no message text. It is our evidence that the erasure happened, and no app can read it.

What deletion removes

Your sign-in account is deleted immediately. That automatically starts a server-side job which removes:

  • your profile record, and with it your name, email, category, country, city, usual hall, games, photo link and player search setting;
  • your player card;
  • the guests' copies of the open games you posted;
  • your profile photo file in Cloud Storage;
  • your notification tokens, notification preferences, activity feed, saved searches, starred venues, weekly schedule and blocked list;
  • your follows of other players, and other players' follows of you;
  • the ratings other players gave you;
  • every match you hosted and every open challenge you posted, along with the requests other players made to join them;
  • every join request and challenge claim you made on someone else's post;
  • your looking-for-a-game post, if you have one;
  • every direct challenge you sent or received;
  • every quick message you sent or received, in any match, and your read marks;
  • the notifications in other players' activity feeds that name you, such as the one telling a host you asked to join their match;
  • your venue check-ins;
  • your feedback messages;
  • the abuse reports you filed about other people.

The job normally finishes within moments. If a step fails, a scheduled task retries the remaining steps every six hours until they are done.

What survives deletion, and why

We would rather be exact here than reassuring, so please read this part.

  • Matches you only joined stay on the host's record. The post belongs to the other player, so we do not destroy it. It keeps the display name, category and user id you had at the time. Your photo file is deleted, so the picture stops loading. If you want your name removed from those records too, email us and we will do it.
  • Ratings you wrote about other players stay, because they are the other player's reputation and that player did not ask for anything. We sever the link to you: the author field is overwritten with the fixed value [erased], which is the same value for everyone, so your ratings cannot be picked out or grouped afterwards.
  • Open challenges you claimed but did not host stay, with your name and id cleared, and the slot reopens.
  • Abuse reports filed about you stay, as a safety record. No app can read them.
  • The erasure record described under retention stays.
  • Server logs may contain your user id for a bounded period.

We do not claim that deleting your account erases every trace of you everywhere. The list above is what remains.

Τη γράφουμε επίσης στην κάρτα παίκτη σου, στα ματς, στις προκλήσεις, στις δηλώσεις ότι ψάχνεις παιχνίδι, στις αποθηκευμένες αναζητήσεις και στο εβδομαδιαίο πρόγραμμά σου, ώστε τα ματς, οι αίθουσες, οι κατατάξεις και οι περισσότερες ειδοποιήσεις κάθε χώρας να μένουν σε αυτήν. Το email σου είναι ιδιωτικό. Το όνομα, η κατηγορία και η φωτογραφία σου εμφανίζονται στα ματς που δημοσιεύεις ή στα οποία συμμετέχεις, όταν δηλώνεις ότι ψάχνεις παιχνίδι, και στην κάρτα παίκτη σου, μαζί με τη χώρα και την πόλη σου, την αίθουσα όπου παίζεις συνήθως και τι παίζεις. Την κάρτα σου τη βλέπουν όσοι έχουν λογαριασμό, και μπορούν να σε βρουν με το όνομά σου, εκτός αν κλείσεις το «Να με βρίσκουν στην αναζήτηση» στις Ρυθμίσεις. Την αίθουσα όπου παίζεις συνήθως την αλλάζεις ή τη βγάζεις από τις Ρυθμίσεις, στο «Η λέσχη σου». Όλα αυτά τα βλέπουν μόνο όσοι έχουν συνδεθεί στην εφαρμογή, με μία εξαίρεση: όποιος έχει τον σύνδεσμο της φωτογραφίας σου μπορεί να την ανοίξει και χωρίς σύνδεση. Από την έκδοση 2.3.0 της εφαρμογής, τα ματς σου και τις δηλώσεις σου ότι ψάχνεις παιχνίδι, μαζί με το όνομα, την κατηγορία και τη φωτογραφία σου, τα βλέπουν και οι επισκέπτες, δηλαδή όσοι ρίχνουν μια ματιά στην εφαρμογή χωρίς λογαριασμό. Την κάρτα σου δεν τη βλέπουν, ούτε όσα γράφεις σε σημείωση: η εφαρμογή δείχνει τη σημείωση ενός ματς ή μιας πρόκλησης, και τη σημείωση μιας ακύρωσης, μόνο σε παίκτες με λογαριασμό. Οι εκδόσεις 2.3.0 έως 2.3.2 διαβάζουν τα ίδια τα ματς και τις προκλήσεις και κρύβουν τη σημείωση στην οθόνη. Ένα αντίγραφο σβήνεται μόλις κάποιος πάρει το ματς ή την πρόκληση, μόλις ακυρωθεί ή διαγραφεί, και όταν ο διοργανωτής διαγράψει τον λογαριασμό του. Αλλιώς, το αντίγραφο ενός ματς σβήνεται μέσα σε περίπου μία ώρα αφού περάσουν δύο ώρες από την έναρξή του, και το αντίγραφο μιας πρόκλησης μέσα σε περίπου μία ώρα αφού συμπληρώσει 24 ώρες ή περάσουν δύο ώρες από την ώρα της. Οι κανόνες ασφαλείας της βάσης μας όμως επιτρέπουν ακόμη σε κάθε συνδεδεμένο χρήστη, και στον επισκέπτη, να διαβάσει ολόκληρη την εγγραφή ενός ματς ή μιας πρόκλησης, μαζί με τη σημείωση, αν τη ζητήσει απευθείας από τη βάση. Θα κλείσουμε αυτή τη δυνατότητα στους κανόνες μόλις οι επισκέπτες προλάβουν να ενημερώσουν την εφαρμογή, και τότε θα το γράψουμε εδώ.

Από την έκδοση 2.3.0 της εφαρμογής, μπορείς να ρίξεις μια ματιά πριν φτιάξεις λογαριασμό, με το «Ρίξε πρώτα μια ματιά» στην πρώτη οθόνη. Οι προηγούμενες εκδόσεις δεν έχουν λειτουργία επισκέπτη. Τότε η εφαρμογή συνδέεται ανώνυμα στο Firebase Authentication και παίρνει ένα τυχαίο αναγνωριστικό επισκέπτη, χωρίς όνομα, email, κωδικό ή προφίλ. Ως επισκέπτης βλέπεις τα ανοιχτά ματς, τις σελίδες τους και τις αίθουσες, αλλά όχι τις σημειώσεις των διοργανωτών. Στη σελίδα ενός ματς, αντί για τη σημείωση, βλέπεις «Η σημείωση του διοργανωτή είναι για παίκτες με λογαριασμό». Για να δημοσιεύσεις, να ζητήσεις να μπεις σε ματς, να αποκλείσεις παίκτη ή να λαμβάνεις ειδοποιήσεις χρειάζεσαι λογαριασμό. Μπορείς όμως να κάνεις αναφορά για έναν παίκτη από τη σελίδα ενός ματς, από μια δήλωση ότι ψάχνει παιχνίδι ή από μια πρόκληση, και η αναφορά κρατά το αναγνωριστικό επισκέπτη στη θέση του αναγνωριστικού χρήστη. Μπορείς επίσης να μας στείλεις σχόλια από την εφαρμογή, που κρατούν κι αυτά το αναγνωριστικό επισκέπτη στη θέση του αναγνωριστικού χρήστη. Όταν φτιάξεις λογαριασμό, αυτός έχει δικό του αναγνωριστικό. Το αναγνωριστικό επισκέπτη μένει στο Firebase Authentication μέχρι να το διαγράψουμε: προς το παρόν δεν σβήνεται αυτόματα, ούτε όταν φτιάξεις λογαριασμό.

Μια προσωπική πρόκληση τη βλέπετε μόνο εσείς οι δύο, μαζί με τη σημείωσή της. Σε Android και iPhone, η εφαρμογή ενημερώνει την εγγραφή του σε κάθε εκκίνηση. Η ώρα της τελευταίας ενημέρωσης, μαζί με την έκδοση της εφαρμογής, μας δείχνει, μόνο ως συνολικούς αριθμούς, πόσοι από όσους άνοιξαν πρόσφατα την εφαρμογή την έχουν ενημερώσει, ώστε να ξέρουμε πότε μπορούμε να βγάλουμε μια αλλαγή. Νομική βάση και γι' αυτό είναι το έννομο συμφέρον μας, και μπορείς να αντιταχθείς όποτε θέλεις. Η έκδοση της εφαρμογής που κρατάμε στον λογαριασμό σου συνδέεται με αυτόν. Και στις δύο εφαρμογές (στο iPhone από την έκδοση 2.2.0), αν επιτρέψεις την τοποθεσία, η κατά προσέγγιση τοποθεσία σου χρησιμεύει για να βλέπεις πρώτα τις αίθουσες και τα ματς της πόλης σου, και η εφαρμογή δεν μας στέλνει ποτέ τη θέση σου. Στο Android, αν επιτρέψεις την τοποθεσία, ο χάρτης των αιθουσών ανοίγει στην περιοχή σου, οπότε το Google Maps φορτώνει τον χάρτη γύρω σου. Το άνοιγμα του χάρτη στέλνει στη Google τη διεύθυνση IP της συσκευής και την περιοχή που βλέπεις. Από την έκδοση 2.4.0, για να διαλέξει ποιας χώρας τα ματς βλέπει ένας επισκέπτης και ποια χώρα προτείνει η εγγραφή, στο Android η εφαρμογή κοιτάζει τη χώρα του δικτύου κινητής τηλεφωνίας όπου είναι συνδεδεμένο το τηλέφωνο, αλλιώς της κάρτας SIM του, αλλιώς της γλώσσας του, και στο iPhone τη ρύθμιση «Περιοχή». Τίποτα από αυτά δεν χρειάζεται άδεια. Η εφαρμογή δεν μας στέλνει το δίκτυο, την κάρτα SIM ή τη γλώσσα που διαβάζει: η εφαρμογή ενός επισκέπτη ζητά από τη βάση μας μόνο τα ματς εκείνης της χώρας, και το προφίλ σου κρατά μόνο τη χώρα που επιβεβαιώνεις. Η τοποθεσία που συνδέεται με τον λογαριασμό σου είναι αυτή που μας δίνεις εσύ. Την πόλη που διαλέγεις για το προφίλ σου και τη χώρα της τις κρατάμε ως κατά προσέγγιση τοποθεσία, συνδεδεμένη με τον λογαριασμό σου. Κρατάμε επίσης τις αίθουσες που δηλώνεις: την αίθουσα όπου παίζεις συνήθως, τις αγαπημένες σου αίθουσες, που σου φέρνουν και τις ειδοποιήσεις τους, τα check-in σου, και την αίθουσα ή την πόλη των ματς, των προκλήσεων, των δηλώσεων ότι ψάχνεις παιχνίδι, των αποθηκευμένων αναζητήσεων και του εβδομαδιαίου προγράμματός σου. Ένα check-in ισχύει 3 ώρες και ο διακομιστής μας το σβήνει μέσα σε περίπου δύο ώρες μετά. Αν αποθηκεύσεις μια αναζήτηση, κρατάμε την πόλη του φίλτρου της, ακόμη κι αν την πρότεινε η εφαρμογή από τη θέση σου.

Διαγραφή λογαριασμού: μέσα από την εφαρμογή (Προφίλ → Ρυθμίσεις → Διαγραφή λογαριασμού) ή με email στο rackupbilliard@gmail.com. Μαζί με τον λογαριασμό σου διαγράφονται η κάρτα παίκτη σου, οι προσωπικές προκλήσεις και τα γρήγορα μηνύματα που έστειλες ή έλαβες, και οι ειδοποιήσεις στη ροή δραστηριότητας άλλων παικτών που αναφέρουν το όνομά σου. Διάβασε παραπάνω τι ακριβώς διαγράφεται και τι παραμένει.
